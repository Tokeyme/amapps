Privacy Policy for the web version of “Podcasts AM”

Last updated: 1 November 2026

This privacy policy explains what data the web version of “Podcasts AM” (the “web version”) processes, which servers your browser connects to when you use it and what rights you have. The web version runs in your browser at podcast.amapps.app and is intended for browsers on computers. It is a limited version of the app: it sells nothing and offers no downloads and no excerpts.

This policy applies only to the web version. The apps on Android, iPhone, iPad and Mac are covered by the app’s privacy policy, and the website amapps.app by the website’s privacy policy (in German); third-party services are governed by their own terms.

2. No Data Protection Officer

No data protection officer has been appointed under Art. 37 GDPR, as there is no legal obligation to do so (individual developer, no large-scale processing of personal data). For privacy questions, please contact the controller named above directly.

3. Key points in brief

  • There is no user account, no registration and no sign-in.
  • The web version sets no cookies and shows no advertising. Apart from the counting with GoatCounter (section 9), it contains no analytics or tracking software. There are no purchases, no purchase page, no rating prompt, no downloads and no excerpts.
  • To measure reach, the web version counts visits and a few events with GoatCounter, without cookies (section 9). No search terms, shows, episodes or other content are included.
  • The web version stores your subscribed shows, playback positions, bookmarks and settings only in your browser (section 10). It does not transmit this data as a whole, not even to the controller. Individual items from it do, however, become visible in the requests a function needs: for example the selected country or – because the page loads cover art when it is opened – which shows you have subscribed to and which show you last listened to (sections 6 to 8).
  • To find and play podcasts, your browser fetches content directly from the internet: from Apple (search, show details, cover art, including the cover art in the charts), from the podcasters’ servers (feeds, audio, images) and from two addresses of the controller, which are hosted by external providers: podcast.amapps.app (the site itself) and daten.amapps.app (charts and additional content).
  • For technical reasons, each server contacted inevitably learns your IP address, the time, the requested address and your browser’s identifier. It can also tell that the request comes from podcast.amapps.app (section 4).
  • The controller does not analyse individual requests and does not try to link them to a person. Of GoatCounter’s counting he sees only aggregated figures, for example by country and referring page (section 9). Through the web version, personal data reaches the controller only if you email him (section 11.1). On his behalf, Cloudflare processes the connection data of requests to daten.amapps.app (section 5.2), and GoatCounter processes the data of the counting (section 9).
  • The controller does not sell any data and does not share any data for advertising or tracking purposes.
  • Where processing is based on legitimate interests, you can object to it at any time (section 16).

4. Connections to the internet – overview

When you use the web version, your browser connects only to the parties listed below. The web version fetches content from them; it does not send forms or files from your device. In addition, it reports visits and a few events to GoatCounter (section 9). Only when you click do amapps.app and, where applicable, a webmail service come in (section 11). What a server learns comes from the request itself – and that includes which content you fetch:

  • Your IP address. No internet connection is possible without it. It can be used to infer roughly which country or region a request comes from.
  • The time of the request.
  • The requested address. It may contain information such as the selected country, your search term or the identifier of a show or episode (details in the following sections).
  • The information your browser sends with every request, above all its identifier (“user agent”) with the browser’s name and version and the operating system, as well as your preferred languages. The web version does not add an identifier of its own. However, the “Origin” header or the referring address (“Referer”) that your browser sends shows that the request comes from https://podcast.amapps.app.

The web version does not send any account information, device or advertising identifier, or crash reports. It sets no cookies; during playback and when loading some images, however, your browser may send cookies that the podcasters’ servers or their service providers have set themselves (section 8).

Security and encryption

The web version fetches the site itself, daten.amapps.app, Apple’s APIs and image servers and GoatCounter over encrypted connections (HTTPS). It always requests feeds over HTTPS, even if the podcaster specifies an unencrypted “http” address. Your browser loads audio and images from the addresses that the podcasters publish. Whether it loads an “http” address unencrypted, switches it to HTTPS or blocks it is decided by your browser. If it loads unencrypted, third parties on the same network (for example on public Wi-Fi) or your internet provider can see which file is being fetched.

Because the web version works without an account and, apart from the counting with GoatCounter, sends no usage data to the controller, the controller – apart from support emails, GoatCounter’s aggregated figures (section 9) and the connection data that Cloudflare processes on the controller’s behalf (section 5.2) – holds no collection of data on the web version’s users that could be lost or stolen.

5. The site podcast.amapps.app and the hosting providers

5.1 The site

When you open podcast.amapps.app, your browser loads the web version: the program code, the graphics engine (CanvasKit), the components for the database in your browser (section 10), icons and the fonts (Roboto, plus Noto fonts for further characters, for example for Chinese, Japanese, Arabic or emoji). The site delivers all of these files itself. It does not load fonts or program components from Google or other providers. The connection data described in section 4 and the requested file are transmitted in the process. In addition, every time it is opened, the site requests its version file (version.json) several times, usually four times, and appends your device’s clock time, so that your browser does not use an outdated copy from its cache. The controller does not run his own server for this; the files are hosted by hosting providers (section 5.2).

6. Podcast directory, search and cover art (Apple)

The web version uses Apple’s public podcast directory. To do so, your browser queries Apple’s interface itunes.apple.com for search and show details and loads cover art from Apple’s image servers (is1-ssl.mzstatic.com to is5-ssl.mzstatic.com). The web version never contacts Apple’s charts interface (rss.marketingtools.apple.com).

  • Charts: The web version does not load the charts for the selected country from Apple, but from an unchanged copy of Apple’s charts on daten.amapps.app that is renewed regularly, usually daily (section 7), because browsers are not allowed to read Apple’s charts directly. The copy contains Apple’s image addresses unchanged; your browser therefore still loads the cover art in the charts from Apple’s image servers.
  • Cover art: Your browser loads the cover art in charts, search, show pages and your library from Apple’s image servers. Every time you open the page, it loads the cover art of the charts and of your subscribed shows, even if another area is currently shown. If you have not subscribed to the show you last listened to, it also loads that show’s cover art for the library. Apple can therefore tell, every time you open the page, which shows you have subscribed to and which show you last listened to. If a show’s feed names no image of its own at all, the image in the large player and in the media controls (section 14) also comes from Apple, including when the web version reopens the player as you open the page; Apple then learns which show is in the player.
  • Search: When you search, your search term is transmitted to Apple together with the selected country as soon as you pause briefly while typing (after about 0.4 seconds), so possibly also an unfinished word, or when you repeat the search. It is used only to show you matching shows from Apple’s directory. The controller does not receive your search terms, and the web version does not store them.
  • Show details: When you open a show, the web version queries Apple with the show’s ID number and the selected country to obtain the address of the podcast feed. This also applies when, on opening the page, it restores the show page you left open last time.
  • Country: Unless you choose a country yourself, the web version picks one based on your browser’s language settings or, failing that, the USA. China is not offered in the web version. It stores your choice in your browser.

The web version does not store Apple’s responses as such. Only details of shows (name, creator, ID number, address at Apple, cover art address) that you have subscribed to, last opened or last listened to are stored permanently (section 10).

  • Recipient: Apple as a separate controller. According to Apple’s privacy policy, Apple Distribution International Ltd. (Ireland) is the controller for users in the European Economic Area; the data is usually stored by Apple Inc., One Apple Park Way, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA. Apple’s Privacy Policy applies.
  • Purpose: displaying search results, show details and cover art, including the cover art in the charts.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR where you trigger the function yourself (search, opening a show, playing an episode or opening it in the large player, changing the country, refreshing the charts). For the requests when the page is opened (cover art of the charts, of your subscribed shows and of the show you last listened to, where applicable the show you last opened and, if the feed names no image of its own, the cover art in the reopened player), Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; the legitimate interest lies in being able to show you the charts, your library and the point where you left off as soon as you open the page.
  • Third country: For users in the European Economic Area, the recipient is Apple Distribution International Ltd. in Ireland. Where Apple passes the data on to Apple Inc. in the USA, Apple states that it relies on the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46 GDPR).
  • Retention: according to Apple’s rules; the controller has no influence over this.

7. Charts and additional content from daten.amapps.app

At daten.amapps.app, the controller provides additional content for selected shows: short summaries, key points, chapters (where applicable with an “Excerpt” marker within the episode) and show notes. For the web version, the copy of Apple’s charts, renewed regularly and usually daily, is also kept there (section 6). No audio files are stored there.

  • When: Every time you open the page, the web version requests the charts for the selected country and the list of covered shows. It also requests the charts when you change the country, when you refresh and on “Try again”, plus a second time if the first response is empty or fails. It requests the list of covered shows again only once the copy held in memory is older than one hour or could not be loaded last time. When you open a covered show, it loads the show’s index and, where applicable, files for individual episodes; for indexes without short texts for the episodes, also as you scroll the episode list, which shows how far you have scrolled. It also loads the index and the episode file when you open an episode in the large player and, if the podcaster provides no chapters, when you play an episode. This also applies when, on opening the page, the web version restores the show you last opened or the player. As a rule, the web version requests nothing there for shows that are not covered. Only if the list of covered shows cannot be read at that moment, although the server is responding, does it send a request when you open a show or episode or play an episode, using an identifier derived from the address of the podcast feed; this identifier, too, allows the show to be determined.
  • What is transmitted: IP address, time, the requested file and the information your browser sends (section 4). The web version adds no identifier of its own and appends no parameters. Because the address of the charts contains the country and the file names contain an identifier of the show or episode, the requests do, however, reveal which country you have selected and which covered shows and episodes you open or play.
  • In the browser: The web version keeps the responses only in memory and never beyond closing or reloading the page. It reuses additional content for at most one hour, and the charts until you refresh them or change the country.
  • Purpose: displaying the charts and providing the additional content.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR where you trigger the function yourself (opening a show or episode, playing an episode, scrolling the episode list, changing the country, refreshing the charts). For the requests when the page is opened, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; the legitimate interest lies in showing you the charts and the additional content without delay as soon as you open the page.
  • Hosting: Cloudflare and GitHub, as described in section 5.2.

8. Podcasters’ servers: feeds, images and playback

The podcasts themselves do not come from the controller. Your browser loads feeds, audio files, chapters and images directly from the servers that the podcasters specify in their feeds. The controller does not provide any of these files himself and receives nothing from these connections.

  • Feed: when you open a show, including when, on opening the page, the web version restores the show you last opened. Some servers do not allow browsers to read their feed. The request still reaches the server; the web version then shows a notice that the show can only be opened in the app.
  • Chapters and images: Your browser loads images of episodes and chapters, and where applicable the podcaster’s own chapter files, directly from the respective server; it loads chapter files when an episode is loaded. If an episode has no image of its own, the web version uses the show’s image from the feed. Your browser loads the image of the episode you last listened to, so often the show’s image, as soon as you open the page, for the bar at the bottom. While the large player is open, it loads chapter images each time playback reaches a chapter with its own image; this reveals roughly how far you have listened. The image servers can tell which images are being fetched from your IP address.
  • Playback: Your browser plays episodes with its own audio player directly from the podcaster’s server. It requests the file in parts (range requests): when you resume an episode or skip within it, it requests the file from that point. The podcaster’s server and every intermediate service (see below) can therefore tell roughly where you resume and where you skip to. The web version does not create a cache of its own for this. Episodes cannot be downloaded in the web version.
  • Cookies of these servers: The web version sets no cookies. However, it loads the audio without a “crossorigin” attribute. Your browser therefore sends, as far as it allows third-party cookies, cookies that the podcaster’s server or an intermediate service has set itself. The same can happen with images that your browser loads as an ordinary image element as a fallback, because the image server does not allow the site to read them directly.
  • Podcasters’ service providers: Many podcasters route the addresses of their episodes through audience measurement or redirect services (for example Podtrac) and have their files delivered by hosting services (for example Libsyn, Megaphone or Podigee). These services then also receive the request. Which services are involved is decided by the podcaster, not by the controller.
  • Recipients: the operators of the podcast servers and their service providers as separate controllers. They are located worldwide; their own privacy terms apply, over which the controller has no influence.
  • Purpose: displaying and playing the content you choose.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the function you requested). For the requests when the page is opened (where applicable the feed of the show you last opened, the image of the episode you last listened to), Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; the legitimate interest lies in showing you straight away the point where you left off.
  • Third country: The servers may also be located outside the EU, including in countries without an adequacy decision. Your browser fetches the content from them directly, just as when you visit a website; the servers receive only the information contained in the request itself (section 4), including the range information and, where applicable, their own cookies (see above). The controller does not pass any further data on to them; in the controller’s view, this therefore does not constitute a transfer by the controller to a third country. You choose the content yourself. The controller has no contracts with the podcasters or their service providers.
  • Retention: according to the rules of the respective providers.

9. Analytics with GoatCounter

Reach is measured with GoatCounter – without cookies and without cross-device recognition. The web version does not load a script from GoatCounter for this; it reports each count itself with a single request to podcastam.goatcounter.com. The following are counted:

  • the visit: once per page load, as soon as the page is actually shown;
  • at most four events, each at most once per page load: that a search was made, that a show was subscribed to, that something was played and that a show can only be opened in the app because its server blocks browsers (“feed blocked”).

The web version does not count while the page is only being pre-rendered, when it runs inside another site’s frame, when the browser is controlled by a program or when you have switched off counting (section 16).

Each count carries the language in which the web version is displayed; it follows from your browser’s language settings. No search terms, names of shows or episodes or other content are included. Also recorded are the referring page (only with the visit and only if your browser provides it), browser and operating system, the approximate screen size (for this, the web version transmits the width of your screen) and the country derived from the IP address, plus the region for requests from the USA, Russia and China. GoatCounter derives browser and operating system from the browser identification. GoatCounter does not evaluate the list of preferred languages your browser sends. The IP address and the full browser identification are not stored; they are held in memory for at most eight hours so that repeated page loads within one visit are not counted twice. GoatCounter does not store individual page views for this site; evaluation happens in aggregate form. As neither the IP address nor the full browser identification is retained, attribution to individual people is not provided for. The figures are not public; only the controller has access to the GoatCounter account and views them after signing in. The data is held on servers of Hetzner Online GmbH (Gunzenhausen, Germany) in Finland and Germany, which GoatCounter uses as a service provider for this; GoatCounter names no other recipients.

  • Recipient: GoatCounter.com, operated by Martin Tournoij, Ireland, as a processor. GoatCounter’s privacy policy applies.
  • Purpose: finding out whether and how the web version is used. The web version is a market test of whether an offering in the browser is worthwhile.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; the legitimate interest lies in a data-minimising analysis of usage. You can object to the counting at any time and also switch it off yourself (section 16).
  • Third country: no transfer to a third country. GoatCounter is operated from Ireland, and the data is held in Finland and Germany.
  • Retention: GoatCounter keeps the aggregated figures without a fixed period; no automatic deletion is set. If the account is deleted, GoatCounter states that data may remain in backups for up to 30 days. What is held in memory to recognise a visit is kept for at most eight hours.

Individual items from it do, however, feed into the requests described in sections 6 to 8: above all the selected country. In addition, the requests for cover art when the page is opened reveal which shows you have subscribed to and which show you last listened to. The stored data does not include an account, your name, your email address or a device identifier.
